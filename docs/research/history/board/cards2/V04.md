Why: the app's descriptor count went from 44 to 54 over the first real run and the run could not say what the growth was;
the control service's connections were among the open candidates. This card adds a test only; no source changes.

What the test proves (`Tests/ControlProtocolTests/ControlListenerDescriptorTests.swift`, `@Suite(.serialized,
.timeLimit(.minutes(1)))`, real listeners on sockets in a short private folder under `/tmp`):
- `theListenerClosesTheConnectionOnThisPath(path:)`, seven paths, each ending with the client reading the end of file (only
  the server's close brings it): a normal call then the client closing; a client of another user; a client past the
  connection limit; a request longer than the limit (after its -32600 answer); a silent client past a short read timeout; a
  connection out of requests; a connection open when the listener stops.
- `manyMixedConnectionsLeaveNoDescriptorBehind`: 131 connections over the paths; the `/dev/fd` count after (minimum of five
  reads) is within 32 of the count before. A leak of one descriptor per connection would show about +128; the slack absorbs
  other suites, which `.serialized` does not stop.
- Clients are non-blocking with a 10 s stall limit, so a server that never closes fails as a time-out, never a hang.
- Checked during development by mutation: removing either of the listener's two closes, or leaking one descriptor per
  connection, made the suite fail.
